What HR teams in China should know

13 statutory days since 2025

China increased its statutory public holidays from 11 to 13 days in 2025: Chinese New Year's Eve became a holiday, and Labour Day became two days.

Expect longer breaks and make-up working days

Each year the State Council turns these statutory days into longer breaks (like the Spring Festival and National Day "Golden Weeks") by moving weekend days. Employees then work on some Saturdays or Sundays to make up for it. The official 2027 schedule usually comes out in November 2026, so plan leave around it.

Working on a statutory holiday

Under China's Labour Law, employees who work on a statutory public holiday must be paid at least 300% of their normal wage for that day.

Holidays for some groups only

Some days are holidays only for certain people: a half day for women on International Women's Day (8 March), a half day for young people aged 14 to 28 on Youth Day (4 May), and Children's Day (1 June) for children under 14.

Frequently asked questions

How many public holidays does China have in 2027?

China has 13 statutory public holiday days in 2027: New Year's Day (1), Spring Festival (4), Qingming (1), Labour Day (2), Dragon Boat Festival (1), Mid-Autumn Festival (1) and National Day (3).

When is Chinese New Year 2027?

Chinese New Year falls on Saturday 6 February 2027. The statutory Spring Festival holiday runs from New Year's Eve on Friday 5 February to Monday 8 February.

When will the official 2027 holiday schedule be released?

The State Council usually publishes the next year's holiday schedule, including make-up working days, in November.

How much do employees earn for working on a public holiday in China?

At least 300% of their normal daily wage, under China's Labour Law.
